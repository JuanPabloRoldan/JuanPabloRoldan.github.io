### About me
I am a first-year Ph.D. student in aerospace engineering at Embry-Riddle Aeronautical University, working in the Computational Fluids and Aerodynamics Laboratory.
My primary research is on shock-induced droplet deformation and aerobreakup, with a focus on how entrapped non-condensable gas and seeded cavitation nuclei change which surface instability wavelengths get selected — simulated using MFC, an exascale-ready multiphase flow code.
I hold a dual M.S. from ERAU and Universidad del Valle, where I remain part of the IMPETUS INDOMITUS group, and I have spent summers at NASA Ames working on hypersonic aerothermodynamics for the Dragonfly aeroshell and at DLR Cologne on reduced-order modeling for compressor cascade optimization.
Outside of research I dance, play guitar, take photographs, and spend an unreasonable amount of time planning trips.
